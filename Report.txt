## Analysis of Results

### 1. Which algorithm produced lower average waiting time?
**Priority Scheduling** produced lower average waiting time (7.33 vs 8.67). 
This is because high-priority processes (priority 1) are served immediately, 
reducing their waiting time significantly.

### 2. Which algorithm produced lower average response time?
**Round Robin** produced much lower response time (4.00 vs 7.33). 
The time-slicing nature of RR ensures every process gets CPU access quickly,
making it more responsive for interactive users.

### 3. Did higher-priority processes gain significant advantage?
Yes. In Priority Scheduling, P3 (priority 1) finished much earlier than 
low-priority processes, demonstrating effective service differentiation.

### 4. Did Round Robin appear more balanced?
Yes. Round Robin gave each process roughly equal CPU time, as seen in the 
Gantt chart where processes alternate execution.

### 5. Was starvation observed?
In this specific test, starvation did not occur because there were no 
continuous high-priority arrivals. However, if more high-priority processes 
kept arriving, low-priority processes could starve under Priority Scheduling.

### 6. How did the quantum affect results?
With quantum=3, Round Robin provided good balance. A smaller quantum would 
improve response time further but increase context switching overhead.

### 7. Recommendation
For mixed workloads with interactive users: **Round Robin** (better response time)
For systems with urgent/important tasks: **Priority Scheduling** (better throughput)
For this specific workload, I recommend **Round Robin** because the faster 
response time benefits user experience.
